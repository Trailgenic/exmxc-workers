## Methodology

The doctrine comes from the original [exmxc Four Forces of AI Power](https://www.exmxc.ai/frameworks/four-forces-of-ai-power). The numerical scoring rule below is the implementation proposed for this ranked edition.

| Force | What we judge |
|:---|:---|
| Compute | Control over the infrastructure needed to train and run AI, including consequential chip, manufacturing, software, and deployment bottlenecks. |
| Interface | Control over where people and institutions encounter AI, express intent, and act: consumer surfaces, developer environments, enterprise workflows, and physical systems. |
| Alignment | Authority and capacity to govern what AI may do: permissions, containment, monitoring, interruption, accountability, and recovery. |
| Energy | Control over the electricity, continuity, location, and enabling power infrastructure that determine where AI can operate and expand. |

Each force receives an editorial score from 0 to 100 in five-point steps. We assess scale, indispensability, enforceable control, and the difficulty of substitution. These are lenses on each force, not additional forces or a separate weighted model.

**Power Score = 50% × strongest force + 50% × average of all four forces.**

This gives value to both dominance and breadth. A company can hold major power through one critical bottleneck; controlling several forces adds to that power. Every force is eligible for the dominance component. The strongest force deliberately counts in both terms: its effective weight is 62.5%, with 12.5% for each other force. No force receives a fixed preference, and the formula stays unchanged across all three years.

For example, scores of 80, 60, 40, and 20 produce a Power Score of 65: half of 80 plus half of their average, 50.

| Force score | Editorial anchor |
|---:|:---|
| 0 | No material independent control of this force in the AI ecosystem. |
| 25 | Limited control; substantially dependent on other actors. |
| 50 | Meaningful control, with credible substitutes and material dependencies. |
| 75 | A major control position that others find difficult to replace. |
| 100 | A defining global bottleneck or platform position in this force. |

Intermediate scores express our judgment between these anchors. A maximum is not a claim of invulnerability or total market ownership. The scales are anchored judgments rather than percentiles: multiple companies can occupy a defining position in distinct indispensable parts of the same force. Scores do not have to sum to 100 across companies.

**Ranking rules.** Rank by the unrounded Power Score. Break exact ties by comparing each company’s force scores sorted from strongest to weakest, then alphabetically if all four match. Display one decimal, rounded half up. Equal displayed scores may therefore have different ranks. Decimal places reflect the formula, not statistical measurement precision; small gaps should be read as close calls.

**Company boundaries.** Score operating company groups, consolidating controlled subsidiaries. Alphabet includes Google; Microsoft includes GitHub; SpaceX includes xAI and X; Tesla is separate. Samsung Electronics excludes other Samsung group businesses. Partnerships, investments, and supply contracts do not transfer ownership of a partner’s full stack. Durable access earns credit proportionate to actual control. Announced acquisitions are excluded from current ownership until completed; the forecast does not require unspecified future acquisitions.

**Applying the forces.** A chip’s efficiency alone does not establish Energy control. A vendor’s design verification alone does not establish general Alignment authority. Model reach does not necessarily give the model producer control over downstream permissions. We award credit for distinct capabilities, not repeatedly for the same revenue stream. Energy includes scarce enabling electrical and thermal infrastructure, while recognizing that equipment suppliers do not own their customers’ generation. Alignment evaluates governing authority and institutional operating capacity, not moral virtue or a promise that a model is safe.

**Forecasts.** We assign new force scores for 2027 and 2030 using our central view of deployment, adoption, substitution, and control. Announced capacity, products, and partnerships inform the forecast; they are not treated as already deployed. A rising score can coexist with a falling rank when peers advance faster. The company notes identify the thesis and the development that would change it.

**Selection and research.** This is an editorial selection of 50 consequential companies across the four forces, including public and private businesses worldwide. It is not a mechanically screened census of every possible competitor. The future ranks compare this same cohort, without inventing future entrants. Current company releases, technical documentation, and research were reviewed through 18 September 2026; dated releases and undated current pages are identified below. Company descriptions establish reported capabilities, not independent proof of every marketing claim. The scores and forecasts are exmxc judgments, not numbers published by the cited sources. Sparse disclosure calls for a judgment about control, not automatic exclusion from the index.

## The calls we are making

- Alphabet leads on the combination of infrastructure and interaction. Microsoft’s enterprise authority keeps it close.
- OpenAI reaches fourth in 2027 and third in 2030 as direct interaction turns into delegated work and infrastructure control. That is an execution forecast.
- NVIDIA remains central, but its relative position falls by 2030 as competing compute stacks and broader platforms gain power.
- Huawei becomes a stronger alternative stack. Our forecast assumes progress in both manufacturing and software adoption.
- Enterprise permissions become more important as agents act. Palantir, ServiceNow, SAP, Databricks, and security providers benefit to differing degrees.
- Electricity and infrastructure matter in their own right. Suppliers and generators appear in the same index as model builders because Energy is one of the Four Forces.

## Research and company judgments

All linked materials were reviewed on 18 September 2026. Dates below are publication dates where identified; “current” means an undated or continuously updated page, not a claim that it was published today. Factual premises are followed by our own interpretation and forecast.

### 1. Alphabet

**Research premise:** Google describes TPUs as the infrastructure behind Gemini and its billion-user products. Ironwood is generally available; eighth-generation TPUs are still listed as coming soon. [TPU product and deployment status](https://cloud.google.com/tpu) (Current product documentation).

**Our call:** Own silicon, consumer distribution, cloud operations, and enterprise controls give Alphabet the broadest position. We expect that combination to preserve first place through 2030; new chips enter the forecast, not today’s deployed inventory.

**What would change the call:** Agents displace Google’s discovery surfaces faster than Gemini captures the new interaction.

### 2. Microsoft

**Research premise:** Microsoft’s July earnings release reports more than 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats and Azure annual revenue above $100 billion. [FY2026 Q4 results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2026-Q4/press-release-webcast) (2026-07-29).

**Our call:** Enterprise distribution and authority over work reinforce Azure. We expect Microsoft to become a leading institutional permission layer for agents, retaining second place even as independent model companies grow.

**What would change the call:** Customers move their agent orchestration and identities outside Microsoft’s products.

### 3. Amazon

**Research premise:** Amazon’s Q2 release describes AWS growth, Trainium commitments, Bedrock, and AgentCore capabilities, including tools for agent execution. [Q2 2026 results](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Second-Quarter-Results/) (2026-07-30).

**Our call:** AWS combines infrastructure, custom chips, model distribution, and operating permissions. Power procurement supports expansion. Amazon gains absolute power, but we expect OpenAI to capture enough direct interaction to move ahead by 2030.

**What would change the call:** Custom-chip commitments fail to convert into useful capacity or customers bypass AWS’s agent layer.

### 4. Meta

**Research premise:** Meta reports 3.6 billion daily active people for June and substantial AI infrastructure spending. Its release distinguishes actual quarterly spending from full-year guidance. [Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) (2026-07-29).

**Our call:** Mass consumer distribution gives Meta immediate AI reach. Compute and contracted energy support growth; enterprise authorization remains a weaker force. We expect its score to rise while agent-native competitors climb faster.

**What would change the call:** Meta turns messaging into the dominant environment for commercial agent transactions.

### 5. NVIDIA

**Research premise:** NVIDIA’s August results report $89 billion in quarterly data-center revenue and describe Vera Rubin production, software, and infrastructure partnerships. [Q2 FY2027 results](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027) (2026-08-26).

**Our call:** The leading compute supplier also controls a powerful developer interface. We expect custom silicon and competing software to reduce its relative dominance by 2030, while model platforms gain permissions and direct users. Infrastructure partnerships are not ownership of partners’ electricity.

**What would change the call:** CUDA and NVIDIA systems become even harder to substitute as custom-chip deployments disappoint.

### 6. OpenAI

**Research premise:** OpenAI’s September infrastructure account describes serving products used by more than one billion people weekly across 40 regions. [Scaling storage for one billion users](https://openai.com/index/scaling-storage-one-billion-users-part-one/) (2026-09-11).

**Our call:** Direct user intent is its strategic asset. We forecast rising control of compute, powered capacity, and agent permissions, taking OpenAI to fourth in 2027 and third in 2030. This assumes execution, not automatic ownership of partners’ infrastructure.

**What would change the call:** Agent usage does not translate into durable workflow control, or infrastructure obligations outpace delivery.

### 7. Apple

**Research premise:** Apple’s July results describe the new Siri announced at WWDC26 and another record for its active installed device base. [Q3 FY2026 results](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-reports-third-quarter-results/) (2026-07-30).

**Our call:** Devices and operating-system permissions make Apple a gatekeeper even when another company supplies the model. We expect some interaction to move into cross-platform agents, reducing relative interface dominance by 2030.

**What would change the call:** Siri becomes the default agent for most device actions, preserving Apple’s maximum interface position.

### 8. ByteDance

**Research premise:** ByteDance’s Seed1.8 paper describes work on agents that use tools and graphical interfaces. [Seed1.8 research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20633) (2026-03-21).

**Our call:** Content distribution and recommendation remain powerful interfaces. We forecast growth in compute and agents, but reduced relative interface dominance as intent-driven interaction spreads. This is a group-level judgment, not an assumption of unrestricted ownership of every regional TikTok operation.

**What would change the call:** Agent products capture enterprise permissions at the same scale as its consumer products capture attention.

**Research premise:** Oracle’s current OCI offering spans public, dedicated, hybrid, and multicloud deployments alongside its database environment. [Oracle Cloud Infrastructure](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/) (Current product documentation).

**Our call:** Database permissions and infrastructure demand reinforce each other. We expect Oracle to gain compute and powered capacity while keeping meaningful institutional control; concentration in a few major AI customers limits our forecast.

**What would change the call:** Large AI customers internalize capacity or fail to meet commitments.

### 10. Tencent

**Research premise:** Tencent’s corporate materials describe Weixin/WeChat distribution and its cloud and enterprise services. [Tencent corporate overview](https://www.tencent.com/) (Current company material).
